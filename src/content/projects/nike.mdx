## The problem

At ADO, the Olympic Athletes Association, sports projects were tracked across Excel sheets, emails and loose documents. That process covered a project's entire cycle: budget, activities, execution and accountability for the funds spent.

Operations were slow and hard to control. There was a lot of administrative effort for ADO, coaches and athletes, little visibility into the real state of each project, and plenty of traceability problems.

The most critical part was the accountability reports. If they weren't closed properly and on time, ADO ended up taking on the financial liability. Time was lost chasing documentation, supporting documents were missing and, in extreme cases, a report could take up to three years to close.

There wasn't a single type of user, either. The system had to organize the work of federation administrators, ADO and Plan Olímpico, each with different needs within the same flow.

## The solution

Niké is an internal platform that centralizes that cycle: uploading budgets approved outside the system, making activities viable, structuring projects and generating the documentation needed to account for each expense.

Digitizing was the starting point. The goal was a process that is clearer, more traceable and easier to manage in real time.

## Technical decisions

The platform was organized around the operational and documentary flow. Each stage had to connect with the next to keep continuity between what was budgeted, what was executed and what was reported.

Instead of adding isolated modules, the focus was on structuring a system able to sustain validations, budget control and administrative follow-up without duplicating work.

## Impact

Centralizing the process reduces the scattering of information and improves control over projects that used to depend on loose files and operational memory.

The most important change is in the accountability reports: less manual follow-up, less time lost chasing documentation and a clearer basis for closing processes on time.

## My role

I joined midway through development, when functional definitions and several prototypes already existed but the experience still needed to be consolidated as a system. I worked mostly on the frontend: I took those more exploratory interfaces and brought them to a more coherent, maintainable scheme.

That meant consolidating visual and interaction patterns, tidying up flows that were still open and finishing pending modules at an already advanced stage.

## The hard part

The hard part was joining an advanced project and giving it coherence without taking apart what already existed. Decisions had been made, modules were in development and there was a working base, but everything had to feel like part of the same system.

The challenge was consolidating patterns and closing flows without adding complexity. Finishing screens wasn't enough: the system couldn't feel like yet another bureaucratic layer on top of the process.
