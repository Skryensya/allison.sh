## The problem

At ADO, the Olympic Athletes Association, tracking sports projects happened across Excel sheets, emails and loose documents. That process covered a project's entire cycle: budget, activities, execution and accountability for the funds spent.

That made operations slow and hard to control. ADO, coaches and athletes carried a lot of administrative overhead, there was little visibility into the real state of each project, and plenty of traceability problems.

The most critical part was the accountability reports. If they weren't closed properly and on time, ADO ended up taking on the financial liability. Time was lost chasing documentation, supporting documents were missing and, in extreme cases, a report could take up to three years to close.

On top of that, there wasn't a single type of user. The system had to organize the work of federation administrators, ADO itself and Plan Olímpico, each with different needs within the same flow.

## The solution

Niké was designed as an internal platform to centralize that whole cycle: uploading budgets approved outside the system, making activities viable, structuring projects and generating the documentation needed to account for each expense.

The idea wasn't just to digitize the process, but to make it clearer, more traceable and easier to manage in real time.

## Technical decisions

The platform was organized around the operational and documentary flow. Each stage had to connect with the next to keep continuity between what was budgeted, what was executed and what was reported.

The focus wasn't on adding isolated modules, but on structuring a system able to sustain validations, budget control and administrative follow-up without duplicating work.

## Impact

Centralizing this process reduces the scattering of information and improves the ability to control projects that used to depend on loose files and operational memory.

But the most important change is in the accountability reports: less manual follow-up, less time lost chasing documentation and a clearer basis for closing processes on time.

## My role

I joined the project midway through its development, when functional definitions and several prototypes already existed but the experience still needed to be consolidated into a system. My work was mainly on the frontend, taking those more exploratory interfaces and turning them into a more coherent, maintainable scheme.

That meant consolidating visual and interaction patterns, tidying up flows that were still open and finishing pending modules at an already advanced stage of the project.

## The hard part

The hard part was joining an advanced project and giving it coherence without taking apart what already existed. Decisions had been made, modules were in development and there was a working base, but everything still needed to feel like part of the same system.

The challenge was consolidating patterns and closing flows without adding more complexity. It wasn't just about finishing screens, but about making sure the system didn't feel like yet another bureaucratic layer on top of the process.
