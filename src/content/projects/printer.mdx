## The problem

I had a cheap thermal receipt printer gathering dust on my desk. It occurred to me it would be fun to connect it to the internet, for no practical reason at all. A build that finishes throws me a receipt, the grocery list comes out on paper before I leave the house, a friend writes me something and it shows up, physical, on my desk, without me lifting a finger. This project came out of that idea.

## The solution

What I built underneath is an API. You send it a POST with whatever you want to print (text, a card, a list, a QR code, an image) and it lands in a queue. A tiny agent, running on the machine connected to the printer, listens to that queue over WebSocket and drives the printer over USB using the ESC/POS protocol. On top of that there's a dashboard to watch the queue live, compose prints by hand and hand out access keys.

The first consumer of that API is Ping, the public part of the project. It has its own key, its own usage limit, reCAPTCHA and a per-IP cooldown to stop bots. The API doesn't know Ping exists. To it, it's just another consumer.

## Technical decisions

The most entertaining part was the odd moments between the pieces. A printer has no undo. Once the paper is out, it's out. Almost all of the design went into the edge cases, like what happens if I unplug the printer halfway through a job (it goes back to the queue, no penalty) or if two agents fight over the same cable (they take turns with a kernel lock, nobody cuts a receipt in half).

Each of those questions was a puzzle with a physical reward. If I solved it right, the printer sang. It's one of the few projects where the bug shows up on paper, and so does the success. It all runs on Bun and Postgres.

## Send me something

This is the best part. You can try it right now, with no account and nothing to install. Go to Ping, where every tap and every send has its own sound, more like a game than a form, write something, and within seconds you have it on paper, on my desk. It's the most direct way to say hi. As soon as it prints, I read it.
